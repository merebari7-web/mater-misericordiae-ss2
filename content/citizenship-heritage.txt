Week: One
Topic: Nigerian Constitution
Step l: Meaning  of the Nigerian Constitution
A constitution is the body of fundamental principles and laws by which a country, state, or
organisation is governed. It is regarded as the supreme law of the land because every other law
 derives its authority from it and must conform to it.
Step 2: Origin of the Nigerian Constitution
Nigeria's constitutional development can be traced through a series of colonial and post
independence constitutions, each building on lessons from the previous one:
Clifford Constitution of 1922 — introduced the elective principle for the first time.
Richards Constitution of 1946 — divided Nigeria into three regions (North, East, West).
Macpherson Constitution of 1951 — introduced a central legislature and greater regional
participation.
Lyttleton Constitution of 1954 — established a federal system of government.
Independence Constitution of 1960 — granted Nigeria self-government under a parliamentary
system.
Republican Constitution of 1963 — made Nigeria a republic with an indigenous Head of State.
1979 Constitution — introduced the presidential system of government.
1999 Constitution (as amended) — the current constitution, restoring civilian, presidential
democracy after military rule.
Step 3: Structure of the 1999 Constitution
The 1999 Constitution is organised into Chapters, Parts, Sections and Schedules for ease of
reference and administration.
Chapter I — General Provisions (establishes Nigeria as a Federal Republic and states the
supremacy of the Constitution).
Chapter II — Fundamental Objectives and Directive Principles of State Policy.
Chapter III — Citizenship.
Chapter IV — Fundamental Human Rights.
Chapter V — The Legislature (National and State).
Chapter VI — The Executive (Federal and State).
Chapter VII — The Judicature.
Chapter VIII — Federal Capital Territory and General Supplementary Provisions.
Schedules — containing details such as the list of states, local governments, and legislative lists
(Exclusive, Concurrent and Residual).
Step 4: Features of the Nigerian Constitution
Supremacy — it is the highest law of the land; every other law is subordinate to it.
Written — it is documented in a single written text.
Rigid — it can only be amended through a special, difficult procedure requiring a high majority in
the National and State Assemblies.
Federal character — it recognises Nigeria as a federation of states and provides for power-sharing
between the Federal, State, and Local Governments.
Republican — it establishes Nigeria as a republic with an elected President as Head of State and
Government.
Provision for Fundamental Human Rights and Fundamental Objectives and Directive Principles
of State Policy.
Separation of powers among the Executive, Legislature and Judiciary.

Week: Two
Topic: Fundamental Objectives and Directive Principles of State Policy
Instructional Procedure:
Step l: Meaning
The Fundamental Objectives and Directive Principles of State Policy are the goals and guidelines
contained in Chapter II of the 1999 Constitution which direct the government on how to conduct
the affairs of the state for the general welfare of the citizens. Although not directly enforceable in
a court of law, they serve as a guide to government in law-making, policy formulation and
administration.
Step 2: Political Objectives
These aim at building a united, strong and self-reliant nation. They include:
Promotion of national integration and prohibition of discrimination on the grounds of place of
origin, sex, religion, status, ethnic or linguistic association.
Encouragement of inter-marriage among persons from different places of origin and the formation
of associations that cut across ethnic, linguistic and religious lines.
Ensuring that sovereignty belongs to the people and that government derives its powers and
authority from the people.  
Guaranteeing participatory democracy and accountability of government to the people.
Step 3: Economic Objectives
These are geared towards a self-reliant and just economic system. They include:
1. Harnessing the resources of the nation to promote national prosperity and an efficient,
dynamic and self-reliant economy.
2. Ensuring the material resources of the nation are used to serve the common good.
3. Controlling the national economy to secure the maximum welfare, freedom and happiness
of citizens on the basis of social justice and equality.
4. Providing suitable and adequate shelter, food, reasonable minimum wage, old age care,
unemployment and sick benefits. 
Step 4: Social Objectives
These aim at building a free and democratic society founded on justice and equality. They include:
1. Ensuring that the state's social order is founded on ideals of freedom, equality and justice.
2. Providing adequate medical and health facilities for all persons.
3. Ensuring equal and adequate educational opportunities at all levels.
4. Protecting the family as the basic unit of society and safeguarding the welfare of children,
young persons and the aged against exploitation.
iv. Educational Objectives
Education receives special attention as a key tool for national development. Government is
directed to:
Direct its policy towards ensuring there are equal and adequate educational opportunities at all
levels.
Promote science and technology.
Eradicate illiteracy, and, as far as possible, provide free, compulsory and universal primary
education, free secondary and free university education.
Encourage adult education and the education of women and out-of-school youths.

Week: Three (3)
Topic: Separation of Power
Instructional Procedure:
Step l: Meaning of Separation of Power
Separation of power is the division of government responsibilities into distinct branches to limit
any one branch from exercising the core functions of another. It was propounded by the French
philosopher Baron de Montesquieu, who argued that liberty is best protected when the legislative,
executive and judicial powers are vested in different, independent bodies.
Step 2: The Executive
The Executive arm is responsible for implementing and enforcing the laws made by the legislature.
At the federal level, it is headed by the President, assisted by the Vice President and Ministers; at
the state level, by the Governor and Commissioners.
Implements government policies and laws.
Maintains law, order and defence of the country.
Conducts foreign affairs and appoints public officials.
Step 3: The Legislature
The Legislature is the law-making arm of government. At the federal level it is the National
Assembly, made up of the Senate and the House of Representatives; at the state level it is the
House of Assembly.
Makes laws for the peace, order and good government of the country.
Approves the national/state budget.
Oversees and checks the activities of the Executive through committees and public hearings.
Step 4: .  The Judiciary
The Judiciary interprets the laws and administers justice. It comprises the Supreme Court, Court
of Appeal, Federal and State High Courts, and other courts established by law.
Interprets the Constitution and other laws.
Settles disputes between individuals, and between individuals and government.
Protects the rights of citizens and ensures justice is administered fairly. . Checks and Balances
•  Checks and balances: refer to mechanisms that allow each arm of government to limit or
supervise the powers of the other arms, preventing any single arm from becoming too
powerful.
The Legislature can override a presidential veto and can impeach the President or Governor.
The Executive can veto (decline to assent to) bills passed by the Legislature.
The Judiciary can declare laws made by the Legislature or actions of the Executive unconstitutional
(judicial review).
The Legislature confirms certain executive appointments (e.g., Ministers, judges)

Week: Four (4)
Topic: Public Service and Citizenship
Step l: Meaning of Public Service and Citizenship
Public service refers to the body of government employees (civil servants) who are engaged in the
administration and implementation of government policies and programmes in ministries,
departments and agencies. Citizenship refers to the status of being a recognised member of a state,
which confers rights and imposes duties and responsibilities on the individual towards the state.
Step 2: . Role of the Civil Service
1. Implementing government policies, programmes and decisions.
2. Providing continuity and stability in administration regardless of changes in political
leadership.
3. Advising government ministers/commissioners on policy matters based on technical
expertise.
4. Delivering essential public services such as education, health, security and infrastructure.
5. Maintaining official records and ensuring efficient administration of government business.
Step 3: Duties of Citizens
Just as citizens enjoy rights, the Constitution and civic responsibility require citizens to fulfil
certain duties, including:
1. Obeying the laws of the land and respecting the rights of others.
2. Paying taxes promptly and honestly.
3. Participating in the democratic process, including voting during elections.
4. Protecting public property and the environment.
5. Rendering support to law enforcement agencies and cooperating with public servants
performing lawful duties.
6. Contributing positively to national development and unity.
Step 4: . Challenges Facing Public Service
1. Corruption and misappropriation of public funds.
2. Bureaucratic bottlenecks and delays in service delivery (red-tapism).
3. Inadequate funding, tools and infrastructure for effective service delivery.
4. Poor remuneration and welfare of public servants, leading to low morale.
5. Political interference and lack of continuity when governments change.
6. Inadequate training and manpower development for civil servants.

Week: Five (5)
Topic: Nigerian Heritage Sites
Sub-Topics: UNESCO sites, National museums, Parks
Step l: . UNESCO World Heritage Sites in Nigeria
A heritage site is a place recognised for its cultural, historical, scientific or natural significance and
legally protected for the benefit of present and future generations. UNESCO (United Nations
Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization) maintains a World Heritage List of sites of
outstanding universal value. Nigeria has two sites on this list:
Sukur Cultural Landscape (Adamawa State) — inscribed in 1999; features the Palace of the Hidi
(traditional ruler), terraced fields, and evidence of a once-flourishing iron-smelting industry.
Osun-Osogbo Sacred Grove (Osun State) — inscribed in 2005; a dense forest along the banks of
the Osun River, regarded as the abode of the river goddess Osun, and one of the last remaining
sacred groves that used to be common in Yoruba culture.
Step 2: National Museums
National museums are government-established institutions responsible for collecting, preserving,
studying and displaying objects of historical, cultural and artistic importance.
National Museum, Lagos — houses the famous Nok terracotta figures and Benin bronzes.
National Museum, Jos — one of the oldest, established in 1952, known for its ceramics and pottery
collections.
National Museum, Benin City — preserves artefacts of the ancient Benin Kingdom, including
bronze and ivory works.
National Museum, Kaduna — houses ethnographic materials representing Northern Nigerian
cultures.
Functions: preservation of artefacts, research and education, promotion of tourism, and
strengthening of national identity and pride.
Step 3: National Parks
National parks are protected natural areas set aside by government to conserve wildlife, plants and
natural landscapes for ecological balance, research, education and tourism.
Yankari National Park (Bauchi State) — known for elephants, warm springs and diverse wildlife.
Cross River National Park (Cross River State) — home to rich tropical rainforest and endangered
species such as gorillas.
Kainji Lake National Park (Niger/Kwara States) — Nigeria's first national park, established around
Kainji Lake.
Gashaka-Gumti National Park (Taraba/Adamawa States) — the largest national park in Nigeria,
noted for chimpanzees and mountainous terrain.

Week: Six (6)
Topic: Traditional Institutions
Sub-Topics: Roles of Chiefs, Kings, Emirs, Obas, Obis  
Step l: Meaning of Traditional Institutions
Traditional institutions refer to indigenous systems of authority and governance headed by
traditional rulers who derive their positions through inheritance, custom or communal selection,
rather than through modern electoral processes. They have existed in Nigeria since pre-colonial
times and remain custodians of culture and tradition.
Step 2: Types of Traditional Rulers in Nigeria
1. Emirs — traditional rulers found mainly among the Hausa-Fulani in Northern Nigeria (e.g.,
the Emir of Kano, Emir of Katsina).
2. Obas — traditional rulers among the Yoruba people in South-Western Nigeria (e.g., the
Oba of Benin, Ooni of Ife, Alaafin of Oyo).
3. Obis — traditional rulers among the Igbo people in South-Eastern Nigeria (e.g., the Obi of
Onitsha).
4. Chiefs — a general title for traditional rulers or title holders found across many ethnic
groups, including the Niger Delta and Middle Belt regions.
Step 3: Roles of Traditional Rulers
1. Custodians of culture — preserving and promoting the customs, traditions and values of
their people.
2. Maintenance of peace and order — settling disputes and conflicts within their domain
through traditional councils.
3. Advisory role — advising government on matters affecting their communities, including
security and development.
4. Mobilisation of the people — rallying communities for developmental projects,
government programmes, and social causes (e.g., immunisation campaigns, census).
5. Symbol of unity — serving as a unifying figure who commands loyalty and respect across
the community regardless of political affiliation.
6. Religious and ceremonial functions — presiding over cultural festivals, rites of passage,
and traditional ceremonies.
